Central Question and Limitations¶

  • What role do wetlands play in wildfire movement across the landscape?
  • Data limitations: restricted to fires with available spatial data in the form of a vector perimeter, begining with the year 2025

Goals¶

  • Determine how much overlap in wildfire area and wetland area exists
  • Determine how many wildfires occured within 5 kilometers of a wetland

Initial Analytical Application¶

To address our central question “What role do wetlands play in wildfire movement across the landscape?”, we have chosen to use spatial analysis. This fits our data, because both the wetlands data from the National Wetlands Inventory and the CalFires Fire Perimeters datasets have associated footprints. Spatial analysis also fits well with our central question, because we want to know where and how much overlap there is between wildfires and wetlands.

We began our analysis with a simple examination of both sets of data (NWI wetlands and CalFire Fire Perimeters), learning about the spatial and temporal distribution of the data. We then used the package Geopandas to perform a nearest neighbor spatial join to determine the distance between each wetland observation and the nearest fire observation. Next, we performed a spatial join between our datasets to obtain the area of wetlands burned in 2025 and determined that there were 1,134 wetlands that experienced a fire in 2025. This join was validated using nearest neighbor data - the number of wetlands with a distance of 0 to the nearest fire matched the number of intersecting records following the spatial join. Finally, we buffered the wetland dataset by 5km and performed another spatial join to determine the area burned within 5 kilometers of a wetland and determined that there were 27,514 wetlands that had a fire within 5 kilometers.

To include a robustness comparison, we include not only the amount of area burned as hectares, but also as a percentage of total wetland area and total fire area.
